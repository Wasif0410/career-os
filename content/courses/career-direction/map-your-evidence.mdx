## Make your experience visible

An evidence map connects a requirement to something you actually did. It can include coursework, employment, a club, caregiving, a hobby or a project. A technical internship is useful experience, but it is not the only place to practice responsibility, communication or problem solving.

For each repeated task from your target postings, write four things: the situation, your action, a result or observation, and a piece of evidence. Evidence might be a demo, a report, a pull request, a teammate's feedback or a specific story you can explain. Keep private or confidential work private; a sanitized description can still establish your contribution.

Classify each row as **ready to explain**, **needs a clearer example** or **needs practice**. This is a working inventory, not an employability score. A blank row tells you where to learn; it does not tell you that you are unqualified for every role.

## A worked example

Requirement: work with teammates. Maya organized a four-person class project. Her action was to split integration tasks and document the API contract. Evidence: an issue thread and the final integration notes. Her explanation includes a mistake: two people assumed different date formats, so she added examples to the contract and a regression test.

This story demonstrates collaboration and judgment. “Great team player” gives a reader much less to evaluate. The team shipped the project; Maya should describe her part rather than claim all of its work.

## Your turn · 30 minutes

1. Create five rows from your target's repeated tasks.
2. Add one real experience to each row you can support. Leave other rows blank.
3. For two rows, write what *you* did and what someone could inspect or ask you about.
4. Pick one gap that would improve your readiness more than polishing an already strong row.

**Leave with:** an evidence map and one priority. Ask a peer to question a row. If your explanation depends on vague words such as “helped,” add the actual action. Bring uncertain priorities to a human coach when you need individualized advice.
