## Start with a question you can test

You do not need to decide your entire career before applying for an internship. You need a useful starting hypothesis: a kind of work, a rough time window and a reason to investigate it. A target gives your learning a purpose and makes it easier to decide which experiences belong on your resume.

Separate **role** from **domain**. Backend engineering is a role; public transport is a domain. Someone can enjoy building APIs while caring most about education, games or climate. You can explore both without claiming expertise you do not have.

Look at three real entry-level postings in one role family. Record the date, location and eligibility requirements. Read for tasks, not just technology names. Do people build interfaces, investigate data quality, maintain services or talk with customers? Note which requirements repeat and which appear in only one posting. A small sample suggests questions; it does not describe the whole market.

## A worked example

Maya is interested in backend internships. Her three postings mention APIs, databases and team collaboration. She has a class API project and experience coordinating a campus event. Her current target becomes:

> I want to explore backend internships for next summer, especially on products used by students. I can show API work, but need stronger evidence that I can test and explain a database decision.

This is more useful than “I need to learn every language.” It names a gap she can act on. It can change after she learns what the work is actually like.

## Your turn · 20–30 minutes

1. Find three relevant postings. If recruiting is closed, use recent archived examples and label their dates.
2. Write down repeated tasks, eligibility constraints and one question you have about the day-to-day work.
3. Draft your target sentence using Maya's structure. Keep uncertainty visible.
4. Choose one experience you already have that supports it, and one gap worth investigating.

**Leave with:** a target hypothesis and three annotated postings. Before moving on, check that your sentence describes work you want to try, rather than only a prestigious company name.
