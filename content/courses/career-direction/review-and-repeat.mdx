## Close the learning loop

After two weeks, review what happened before adding more tasks. Compare your deliverable with the definition of done. Record what worked, what surprised you and what remains uncertain. A completed task can produce useful evidence; an incomplete experiment can still reveal that your scope or assumptions were wrong.

Separate things you control from outcomes you do not. You can improve clarity, test a feature and prepare an explanation. You cannot control every recruiting timeline or whether a particular employer responds. A small batch of rejections is not a reliable diagnosis of your entire career.

Review your target periodically. If the work felt interesting, deepen one skill. If it did not, investigate why: was it the domain, the task, the tool or the working conditions? Try a smaller comparison before abandoning or committing to a role family.

## A worked example

Maya's reviewer could run the API, but did not understand the database setup. Maya fixes the README. She also learns she enjoys debugging more than visual design. Her next cycle focuses on a query performance experiment rather than another frontend framework.

Her reflection has three lines: “The feature works on the documented sample. Setup was confusing until I added seed instructions. I still need to understand how the query behaves with more data.” That gives her an honest project story and a clear next step.
