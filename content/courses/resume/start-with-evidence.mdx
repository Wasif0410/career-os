## Start with the work, then the page

A resume helps someone understand what you could contribute to a particular role. Before choosing a template, collect your evidence. Write down projects, classes, jobs, volunteering and responsibilities. For each, note the problem, your contribution, the tools you used and what changed.

You can use a class assignment, but label it accurately. Explain your own decisions and distinguish them from starter code and teammates' work. A part-time job can show reliability or process improvement even when it is not a software role. Select examples for relevance, not only prestige.

Now choose a simple structure. For many students, contact links, education, relevant experience or projects, and skills are a useful starting point. Put the strongest relevant material where it is easy to find. A one-page draft is a helpful constraint for a student; follow the employer's instructions and your local career conventions when they differ.

## A worked example

Maya has a database project, a campus help-desk job and a tutorial app. For a backend internship she gives the database project more space. She describes the help-desk work with a real troubleshooting example. She keeps the tutorial brief until she can explain an independent improvement.

Her inventory includes links and rough notes before polished bullets. “Wrote a migration script and checked duplicate records” is a stronger starting note than “demonstrated excellence.” The script and the checks provide material she can defend in a conversation.

## Your turn · 30 minutes

1. Inventory five experiences without worrying about wording.
2. For each, name your contribution and one piece of supporting evidence.
3. Select three that relate to your target's tasks.
4. Put them into a plain draft with readable headings and working contact links.

**Leave with:** an evidence-first resume outline. If a claim would surprise your teammate or supervisor, clarify your role before using it. You will improve the writing in the next lesson; do not invent experience to fill whitespace.
