## Review the file someone receives

The editable document is not the finished resume. Export in the format the employer requests, then open that exported file. Check headings, dates, line breaks, contact details and links. A clean single-column layout with real selectable text is a sensible starting point; parsing behavior varies across hiring systems.

Try selecting and copying the text into a plain text editor. Is the order understandable? This is a practical diagnostic, not a guarantee of compatibility with every system. Avoid making essential information depend on decorative icons, graphics or color alone.

Read once for factual consistency and once for clarity. Check dates against your LinkedIn profile, repository names against actual projects and claims against your notes. Use a descriptive filename, keep the source document and record which version you submitted.

## A worked example

Maya's PDF looks good, but her GitHub link points to a renamed repository. One long bullet wraps into the next section. She fixes the link in the source, shortens the bullet and exports again. She asks a classmate to find her strongest project and explain her contribution. The classmate's answer reveals that the heading is still too vague.

The review catches concrete communication problems. It does not certify that an employer will select her.

## Your turn · 20 minutes

1. Export and reopen your tailored resume.
2. Test text selection, reading order and every link.
3. Check names, dates, spelling and ownership of each claim.
4. Ask a reviewer which example best supports your target, then fix one confusing part.

**Leave with:** a checked resume and its editable source. Keep private contact details out of public repositories. Use university career services or a human coach for feedback when your situation needs more context than a checklist can provide.
