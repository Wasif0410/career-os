## Connect your story to evidence

Use experience, education, projects and skills to support the story in your introduction. Include relevant campus, part-time and volunteer work with accurate titles. An experience entry can explain the problem, your contribution and what you learned without pretending it was a professional engineering job.

Choose a small number of useful work samples. A project demo, a case study or a technical explanation gives someone a way to inspect your work. Explain what each link contains and what part you owned. Check that links open and do not require credentials your viewer lacks.

LinkedIn's Featured section can highlight work, but visibility depends on account and profile settings. Keep direct project links available where appropriate and check the experience with another viewer. Do not assume that everyone who sees a public profile can see every featured item.

## A worked example

Maya features a club equipment project. Its description says “Class project; I implemented search and input validation.” The link opens a README with screenshots and setup instructions. She also includes a short case study explaining an unresolved accessibility issue.

The limitation helps a reader understand the current state. She does not call the project a startup or imply that a club adopted it unless that actually happened.
