## Maintain a useful, accurate profile

Update your profile when your work or goals meaningfully change. A useful maintenance session checks dates, links, your headline and whether the evidence you feature still reflects what you want to discuss. You do not need a daily posting schedule to use LinkedIn thoughtfully.

If you share a project update, explain a problem, a decision and what you learned. Credit collaborators and link to something inspectable when you can. A modest, specific reflection is easier to discuss than a broad claim about transforming an industry.

Review visibility settings as a separate task. Public profile and contact information controls affect different things. Inspect who can see or download your email, what your public profile shows and whether you want changes broadcast. Choose the visibility that fits your circumstances.

## A worked example

Maya writes: “My equipment search returned duplicate results when names differed only by case. I added normalization and a regression test. Next I want to check whether this changes how accented names behave.”

She links to the relevant explanation, credits her teammate's reproduction steps and avoids posting real student information. A follow-up question about text handling now has a useful starting point.

## Your turn · 20 minutes

1. Open each important profile link and remove stale entries.
2. Compare dates and project descriptions with your resume.
3. Review public and email visibility using LinkedIn's current settings.
4. Draft one short learning update or a private reflection if you prefer not to post.

**Leave with:** a maintained profile and a clear next update. Ask whether a stranger could understand your current interests without assuming expertise you have not claimed. Profile polish supports your work; it does not replace learning, practice or human feedback.
