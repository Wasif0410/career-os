## Give yourself more to notice

“Don't be square” is an invitation to look beyond your usual technical routine. A hobby, part-time job, campus group, community activity or unfamiliar book can give you questions that a programming tutorial will not. You do not need an impressive lifestyle to become a more observant person.

Choose something you are curious about for its own sake. Pay attention to how people organize work, make decisions and deal with constraints. What do they value? Which tasks are repetitive? Which problems would be made worse by adding software? Domain knowledge begins with understanding a context, including what you do not know.

You do not have to turn every interest into a portfolio project. Some experiences matter because they are enjoyable, help you rest or connect you with people. Reflection can help you recognize what you learned without making every hour a career exercise.

## A worked example

Maya joins a local board-game group. She notices that teaching newcomers is harder than scheduling games. Different explanations work for different people. She practices listening and simplifying rules, then realizes that those skills also matter when explaining her code.

She does not immediately build an app for the group. She first asks whether there is a problem members actually want help with. Being present is more useful than arriving with a solution in search of a user.

## Your turn · one small experience this week

1. Pick an activity outside your usual technical feed: a library event, hobby group, public lecture, conversation or community discussion.
2. Choose a free, remote or self-paced option if that fits your time and access needs.
3. Notice one surprising detail and one assumption you had to revise.
4. Write a question you want to explore further.

**Leave with:** a curiosity note. It can be five sentences. This is not a challenge to become more extroverted or spend money on travel. The goal is to develop attention, perspective and interests that are yours.
