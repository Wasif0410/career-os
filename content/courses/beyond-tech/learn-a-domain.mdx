## Understand the work around the software

Pick one domain you want to understand better: public transport, music, education, agriculture, games or another area that interests you. Start with a narrow workflow rather than trying to master an entire industry. Find out who does the work, who benefits, who makes decisions and what can go wrong.

Create a field guide with five sections: people, workflow, vocabulary, constraints and open questions. Use different kinds of sources. An organization's operational documentation may explain a process; a practitioner interview may reveal exceptions; a public report may show broader context. Note dates and incentives. A vendor's sales page and a user's complaint are perspectives, not complete accounts.

Separate facts from assumptions. When sources disagree, preserve the disagreement and write what would help resolve it. Familiarity with terminology is a starting point, not a claim that you are qualified to make consequential decisions for that field.

## A worked example

Maya explores campus equipment lending. Her workflow is “request, reserve, collect, return, inspect.” She discovers that availability depends on maintenance, not only whether something has been returned. Her vocabulary list includes reservation, checkout and inspection. Her open question is who is allowed to mark an item ready.

That distinction changes her project: a simple available/unavailable flag may hide important information. Understanding the process improves her requirements before she chooses a database schema.

## Your turn · 45–60 minutes

1. Choose one domain and a specific workflow.
2. Consult three sources with different perspectives; record their dates and links.
3. Draft the five sections of your field guide.
4. Mark at least two assumptions and one disagreement or missing perspective.

**Leave with:** a one-page domain field guide. Explain it to someone outside the field using ordinary words. If your explanation is only a list of acronyms, return to the people and the task they are trying to complete.
